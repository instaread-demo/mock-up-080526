Buffalo will be a destination city. Cleveland, too.

Great Lakes cities like ours have access to fresh water and enjoy a temperate climate that will eventually prompt an exodus north as the Southwest bakes.
By Jim Heaney
  • Share on

Water rationing is about to begin in Arizona, Nevada and California.

The Washington Post reports the states “will be required to curb their use of the water from the Colorado River by a combined 20 percent over the next two years — and could ultimately face even larger cuts.”

The combination of water shortages and scorching temperatures – today’s forecast for Phoenix is for a high of 112 – will have long-term implications for Buffalo and other cities on the Great Lakes. We’ve got plenty of fresh water and temperate weather. 

In due time, places like Buffalo, Cleveland and the like will become destinations for climate refugees. In the meantime, the challenge for Great Lakes cities is to get their act together. In Western New York’s case, that starts with cleaning up our government and environment. 

It’s not too early to start planning for that future.

A story in City and State New York asks the question: Should our state Legislature meet year round? 

Lawmakers meet in session from January through June. At $142,000, they’re the highest paid state legislators in the nation and supported by large staffs. There’s a case to be made that they ought to be in session for at least part of the second half of the year, but that’s not the most-significant reform to be had. 

That would be reinvigorating the legislative process, which presently puts too much control in the hands of the leadership of the Senate and Assembly, who in turn negotiate with the governor. (AKA, three men in a room.) The state budget and many key policies are then presented – take it or leave it – to the rank and file at the 11th hour of the legislative process. 

Trump’s war against Iran is good for business at Moog, which reported a big increase in profits in its latest quarter. Contributing to the increase is a push to replenish the military’s stockpile of missiles, for which Moog produces guidance systems.

The war is also good for the big oil companies, which are reporting a huge increase in profits. Exxon’s quarterly profits more than doubled from the previous year to $14.5 billion. Chevron’s quadrupled to $12.1 billion.

The trend to electronic vehicles is unmistakable and probably inevitable. So why is Detroit retreating in favor of gas guzzling trucks and SUVs?

File this under “Let Them Eat Cake.”

Trump has ordered 250 Cadillac Escalades for his presidential motorcade, declaring: “We look sharp in those Escalades.”

At the same time, his administration has announced the elimination of subsidies for Medicare Part D – the government health insurance program’s prescription drug benefit — that will drive up costs for tens of millions of beneficiaries. 

In short, he wants to spend on bombs and Cadillacs, not prescription drugs.

Ah, but it’s not just old people he’s gunning for. His administration is also going after Head Start.

File this under “presidential pettiness on display:”
