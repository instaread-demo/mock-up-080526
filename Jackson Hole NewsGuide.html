Older vaccine was less effective at preventing measles.

Summer camps consider measles policy as Targhee requires shots

Summer camps are reviewing their policies and practices for preventing measles as parents in the Tetons worry about a recent outbreak in Grand Teton National Park.

Grand Targhee is now requiring that all children attending day care and summer kids camps at the resort present proof of vaccination against the highly contagious virus.

“Targhee is requiring proof of vaccinations for day care and kids summer camp, with no exceptions,” a Grand Targhee representative said. “We’re doing this in the best interest of our guests and children.”

It’s unclear the extent to which other summer camps in Teton County are moving in a similar direction. Though Wyoming schools and day care facilities require measles vaccination, with exceptions, similar state rules don’t apply for summer camps, according to Dr. Alexia Harrist, state health officer at the Wyoming Department of Health.

“Similar to school settings, we would highly recommend that children attending summer camps be updated on MMR vaccines,” Harrist said.

None of the five measles cases reported in Teton County were in children. The most recent case was reported on July 14.

Wyoming schools allow parents not to get their children vaccinated if they have a religious or medical exemption, according to Teton County Health Department Director Dr. Travis Riddell. Targhee’s no-exception policy “would be different,” he said.

“Certainly any private organization like that can make their own requirements,” Riddell said.

Other popular Jackson Hole summer camps are taking steps to make sure that parents and staff are prepared for measles.

Teton Science Schools already required measles vaccination for its early childhood campers — children ages 3 to 5 — according to Executive Director Wayne Turner. Under the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s guidelines, those children may only have received their first of two MMR doses, granting them 93% protection against the virus. Local officials recently recommended that children receive their second vaccine earlier, which bumps protection up to 97%.

“We advised all family members, or camper families, that this is an option for them to pursue,” Turner said.

Teton Science Schools has not required vaccinations for campers above 5 years old, Turner said. It was advising parents and staff to be on the lookout for symptoms.

“We don’t know the full status of the vaccinations of everybody that comes on our campuses, we just don’t,” Turner said. “But we have been communicating with all early childhood families and all staff about what to do” if symptoms emerge.

Camp Jackson, which is run by Teton County/Jackson Parks and Recreation, is not currently requiring proof of vaccination, according to Director Tyler Florence. Staff are receiving additional training tailored to measles, he added, including recognizing symptoms, how to implement isolation procedures, and working with local health authorities.

“The safety of our campers and staff is our top priority,” Florence wrote in an email.
