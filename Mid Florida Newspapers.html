    Women United, a United Way program, donated 60 bags filled with fresh fruits, vegetables, and care kits to Nu-Hope Elder Care Services on Wednesday, July 29.

    In a joyful day, Women United volunteers and Nu-Hope staff visited seniors in various locations throughout Highlands County as part of a community effort to improve access to healthy food.

    The volunteers met at the Nu-Hope facility, where they were given a tour before being divided into four groups with staff members. Each team then traveled to different locations to deliver the bags of fresh fruits, vegetables, and essential supplies to seniors participating in Nu-Hope programs.

    This initiative was made possible thanks to the support of United Way donors and community partners. The food was supplied by Double K Farm LLC, in Lorida, strengthening support for local agriculture. In addition, 60 flowers donated by Hobby Hill Design brightened the morning for the seniors, who were not expecting the visit.

    “Many times when you think about our senior population in Highlands County, you think of the snowbirds and not those who are struggling. Since I started working at United Way, I wasn’t aware of how neglected the senior population was,” said Shawn Beumel, Highlands County director of United Way.

    “Most of our seniors have fixed incomes; however, fruits and vegetables are a real benefit to them. They generally don’t buy them because they’re trying to make ends meet with mortgages or other food. So, I’ve learned from this population that we must provide them with good nutrition. You have to make sure they’re moving and get them out of their four walls because that will improve their quality of life. So, this is a true blessing. I’m glad it happened so quickly. We’re excited about our relationship with United Way, but also with our community. I think we’re the county’s best-kept secret,” said Joseph Cox, executive director of Nu-Hope.

    The distribution of the bags of food serves one of the community’s most vulnerable populations, as many of the seniors face difficulties getting around and accessing fresh food regularly. Last week’s visit had a great impact on those who received the food, but also gave them an opportunity to talk to the volunteers.

    “For many older adults, seeing volunteers who genuinely care is every bit as meaningful as the items they receive. Isolation is just as real as hunger. Nu-Hope knows the seniors and their needs. United Way and Women United can bring volunteers, resources and community partners to help meet those needs. We are all better when we work together,” Beumel said.
