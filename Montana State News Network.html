Some Montana Kids Have Never Been to MontanaFair but You Can Help Change That

For a lot of people, MontanaFair isn't just another event on the calendar. It's a tradition that's been part of their summers for as long as they can remember.

Maybe it was the rides. Maybe it was the smell of fresh lemonade and corn dogs. Maybe it was wandering through the barns or leaving with an oversized stuffed animal you somehow convinced your parents to let you win.

Those are the kinds of memories that stick with you. Unfortunately, not every child gets the chance to make them.

That's why I was really happy to see a new effort called the Little Fairgoers Fund. It's a simple idea with the potential to make a big difference for local families.

Why the Little Fairgoers Fund Means So Much for Montana Kids

The Little Fairgoers Fund provides free MontanaFair admission to children ages 6 through 12 who might not otherwise be able to go.

For some families, the cost of admission alone can be enough to keep a trip to the fair from happening. This program helps remove that obstacle, giving more kids the chance to enjoy everything that makes MontanaFair special.

It's about making memories with family, seeing farm animals up close, watching a show, trying something new, and being part of an experience that so many of us take for granted.

Sometimes the Biggest Memories Start With One Ticket

There are plenty of worthy causes to support, but this one really stood out to me. It's easy to forget that something as simple as a trip to the fair isn't possible for every family.

If you've ever watched a child's face light up at the top of the Ferris wheel, during a concert, or while meeting animals for the first time, you already know why this matters.

Helping create one of those memories for a local kid might be one of the easiest ways to make someone's summer a little brighter.

How to Sponsor a Child's MontanaFair Experience

Maybe you own a local business and want to sponsor a group of kids. Maybe your coworkers decide to chip in instead of buying lunch for the whole office one Friday. Maybe your friends, church group, or neighbors want to pool their money to help send a few children to MontanaFair.

However you choose to give, there are sponsorship options for businesses as well as custom contribution amounts for individuals and groups. You can even choose whether the tickets are distributed through local youth organizations or provided to your business to share with families in the community. It's a simple way to help more kids experience the magic of MontanaFair.

Get our free mobile app

Ready to help make a local kid's summer a little brighter? You can learn more about the Little Fairgoers Fund and become a sponsor through the official Little Fairgoers Fund page.

What You Can’t Bring to MontanaFair: Prohibited Items List

Before heading to MontanaFair at MetraPark, check this photo list of prohibited items that must stay home, including additional restrictions for First Interstate Arena.

Gallery Credit: Traci Taylor

Photo by Igor bispo on Unsplash

Weapons Are Not Allowed at MontanaFair

Weapons of any kind are prohibited, including real, toy, or replica firearms, firearm parts, ammunition, and gunpowder. Knives of any size, brass knuckles, blackjacks, kubatons, martial arts weapons, nightsticks, nunchucks, billy clubs, bats, stun guns, Tasers, self-defense sprays, mace, pepper spray, sticks, and poles are also not permitted.

Photo by GRAS GRÜN on Unsplash

Drugs and Cannabis Products Are Prohibited

Illegal drugs and substances are not allowed at MontanaFair. Marijuana and cannabis products of any kind are also prohibited and must be left at home.

Photo by Andres Siimon on Unsplash

Smoking and Vaping Are Not Allowed Inside

Smoking and vaping are prohibited inside the building under the Montana Clean Indoor Air Act. Any cigarettes, e-cigarettes, or vaping devices must be used only in designated outdoor areas, if available.

Photo by Ben Duke on Unsplash

Outside Food, Drinks and Coolers Are Prohibited

Coolers, ice chests and outside food or beverages of any kind are not allowed. Cans and bottles made from plastic, metal, glass or aluminum are also prohibited.

Photo by William Bayreuther on Unsplash

Professional Cameras and Recording Devices Are Prohibited

Professional cameras, including DSLRs, Leicas and GoPros, are not allowed. Cameras with detachable lenses, tripods, monopods, camera sticks and selfie sticks are also prohibited, along with video or audio recording devices such as drones, Meta glasses, iPads and tablets.

Photo by Andrey Matveev on Unsplash

Laptops, iPads and Tablets Are Prohibited

Laptops, iPads, tablets and similar electronic devices are not allowed inside the venue and should be left at home or securely stored before entering.

Photo by Ksenia Varapaeva on Unsplash

Some Bags and Personal Items Are Prohibited

Backpacks and clear bags larger than 12 by 6 by 12 inches are not allowed. Waist packs, blankets, signs, banners, flags, and umbrellas are also prohibited and should be left at home or locked in your vehicle.

Photo by Cecilia Mcaloon wolff on Unsplash

Spiked Jewelry, Aerosol Cans, Glow Sticks and More Are Prohibited

Other prohibited items include sporting equipment, fireworks, pyrotechnics, aerosol cans, silly string, sharp or spiked jewelry, inflatable items, beach balls, laser pointers, glow sticks, and candles. Animals are not allowed unless they qualify as service animals under the Americans with Disabilities Act and venue policy. Bicycles, scooters, Segways, roller skates, roller shoes, strollers, binoculars, and noise-making devices such as whistles, horns, instruments, and bells are also prohibited.

Photo by Kat van der Linden on Unsplash

Diaper Bags and Medical Bags Are Allowed With Inspection

Diaper bags and medical bags larger than 12 by 6 by 12 inches are permitted, but all bags will be subject to inspection before entering the venue.

Getting to Know Billings From A to Z

New to Billings or not, the Magic City may look like an industrial town from the freeway, but take any exit and you'll discover a city with idiosyncrasies and a whole lot of Montana personality.

Gallery Credit: Ashley Warren

Credit: Ashley Warren, Townsquare Media

A - Alberta Bair Theater

Renovated in 2020, the Alberta Bair Theater is host to some of the finest entertainment that makes its way to Billings. Acts include Kristin Chenoweth, Rob Schneider, David Sedaris, and the Billings Symphony among others.

Credit: Google Maps

B - Boothill Cemetery

Many people don't know this but Billings could have been a town called 'Coulson' had the railroad lines been built in a different place. When the railroad set down and Billings developed around it, the town of Coulson faded away. Boothill Cemetery or 'Coulson Cemetery' is associated with the town that no longer exists.

Credit: Michael Foth, Townsquare Media

C - Crystal Lounge and Casino

Fans of karaoke will want to hit up the Crystal Lounge and Casino in the heart of Downtown Billings. As they like to say, "get crystalized."

Credit: Michael Foth, Townsquare Media

D - Dehler Park (Billings Mustangs)

Dehler Park is home of Billings' Mustangs, an Pioneer League independent baseball team. The park replaced Cobb Field, which was started by Billings salad officiando, Bobb Cobb.

Photo by Zachary Keimig on Unsplash

E - Escape Rooms

While Montana may be slow to join trends, we did hop on this one. Billings has two escape rooms, Billings Escape Room and The Grand Escape Room, and both come highly recommended.

Photos by Ann Boucher/BLM

F - Four Dances Recreation Area

On the east side of Billings, the geographic area along the Yellowstone River contains the Four Dances Recreation Area, which is a Special Recreation Management Area. Popular activities include hiking, photography, and birdwatching.

Credit: Michael Foth, Townsquare Media

G - Ghost Dance Exhibit at the Yellowstone County Museum

This impressive exhibit contains clothing and items used in a Ghost Dance. According to the Yellowstone County Museum, the display, on loan from Larry Williams, "is the largest public display of Ghost Dance objects in the world."

Credit: Billings Depot via YouTube

H - Historic Billings Depot

Once the passenger station for the railways, the Bilings Depot is now a popular wedding and event venue. Spanning four blocks along Montana Avenue, the site is perfectly located across from some of Billings' finest restaurants.

Photo by Steve Halama on Unsplash

I - Indian Cliffs

Indian Cliffs is both a neighborhood and mountain biking/hiking/running trail in northwest Billings. While the neighborhood sits atop the Rims, the trail winds through a field before following the Rims west.

Credit: Google Maps

J - Josephine Crossing

One of Billings' favorite neighborhoods, this community developed the nickname "Whoville" because of its dynamic architecture and the personable relationships between the residents. The nickname made its way back to the people of Josephine Crossing...and they embraced it! Now, every year at Christmas you can drive the streets of Josephine Crossing and see Grinch-themed displays that help the neighborhood live up to its moniker.

Photo by Togo RV on Unsplash

K - KOA

The popular and ubiquitous campgrounds actually have their headquarters right here in Billings and the first KOA campground is located in Billings, too.

Credit: Montana Fish Wildlife and Parks

L - Lake Elmo State Park

Because much of the Yellowstone River is not ideal for recreation, Lake Elmo State Park is where Billings residents go for fun in and on the water.

Credit: Ashley Warren, Townsquare Media

M - Magic City

Remember that story about Billings growing with the railroad? It was that quick growth that earned Billings the name, "Magic City."

Photo by Daniel Lloyd Blunk-Fernández on Unsplash

N - The NILE

The Northern International Livestock Exposition (NILE) is a week-long annual event in October where livestock are shown and sold.

Credit: Oscar's Park via Facebook

O - Oscar's Park

A popular spot for weddings, Oscar's Park has, according to wedding-spot.com, BIllings' oldest church steeple. If that's not charming enough, the trees, grass, and views of the surrounding hills and flowing Yellowstone River will certainly make any event feel special.

Credit: Montana Fish Wildlife and Parks

P - Pictograph Cave State Park

Located to the east of Billings, Pictograph State Park offers a short walking trail with displays to view "over 100 pictographs or rock paintings. The oldest rock art in the cave is over 2,000 years old."

Credit: Yellowstone Drag Strip via Facebook

Q - Quarter Mile Yellowstone Drag Strip

Billings loves its classic cars (Burn the Point, anyone?), and of the many ways to celebrate motor culture the Yellowstone Drag Strip is a favorite. The quarter-mile drag strip is locally owned and operated and is NHRA sanctioned.

Credit: Larry Edwards

R - Rimrocks or 'The Rims'

The geographical feature along the north side of Bilings is known as the Rimrocks or 'the Rims." The Billings airport actually sits on top of the Rims and the Rims provide plenty of recreational opportunities--from paved walking paths to mountain bike trails and even rock climbing. And if you look closely, you'll find a set of stairs carved right into the rocks.

Credit: Johnny Vincent, Townsquare Media

S - Softies

While you may have waited for Dairy Queen to open every year in the town where you're from, in Billings, we wait for Softies. And when Softies opens, summer is right around the corner.

Credit: Billings TrailNet via Facebook

T - Billings TrailNet

One of the most talked about fundrasiers in Billings is the annual Ales For Trails event hosted by BIllings TrailNet, an organization whose mission it is to promote a complete community-wide trail system. Currently, Billings has 50 miles of trails.

Credit: Google Maps

U - Montana State University - Billings

The public university situated at the base of the Rims has undergraduate and graduate programs. As of fall 2020, the school had "a total enrollment of 4,000 students...46.4% [who] attended full time and 53.6% [who] attended part-time."

Credit: Google Maps

V - Veronika's Pastry Shop

On Montana Avenue you'll find Veronika's Pastry shop. Its owner, Veronika Baukema, is from Uzbekistan and bakes French, Russian, and Eastern European pastries. And they are delicious!

Credit: Google Maps

W - Western Heritage Center

One of the best ways to learn about south-central and south-eastern Montana is with a visit to the Western Heritage Center. They offer onsite and online exhibits and provide traveling exhibits to fellow Montana businesses and organizations.

Credit: Eric Warren

X - Diamond X Disc Golf and Phipps Park

Disc golf has gotten so popular in Billings that we have disc golf store downtown! Diamond X Disc Golf Course at Phipps Park comes with the most "Montana" advice ever: 'Wear pants, avoid snakes, bring water, be prepared to climb." And of course, enjoy the view.

Credit: Ashley Warren, Townsquare Media

Y - Yellowstone Art Museum

Home to some of the region's most engaging artwork, the Yellowstone Art Museum appeals to patrons across the art appreciation spectrum. Popular annual events include Summerfair, the Masquerade Ball, and the Yellowstone Art Auction

Credit: Townsquare Media Billings

Z - ZooMontana

This site on BIllings' West End is not only home to a variety of animals (like Winston the Sloth and Simpson the Gray Wolf), but it is also host to great annual events like the Montana Renaissance Festival and the Rubber Duck Regatta.
