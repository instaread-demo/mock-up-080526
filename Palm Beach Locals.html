A Brooklyn Pizza Legend Lasted Less Than a Year in West Palm Beach

Juliana’s Pizza was supposed to anchor the NORA district. It was the first expansion outside Brooklyn for Patsy Grimaldi — the man who built Grimaldi’s under the Brooklyn Bridge before a lease dispute pushed him to open Juliana’s next door. The West Palm location opened inside the billion-dollar NORA development with a custom-built coal-fired oven shipped from New York.

It lasted less than one year.

The restaurant closed permanently. The hand-built coal oven is still sitting inside the dark building. The NORA district, which pitched itself as a live-work-eat neighborhood anchored by walkability and local food, now has a vacant anchor tenant space on its main corridor. No official reason was given for the closure. The question residents are asking is not why this restaurant closed — it is what happens to the space next, and whether the NORA district’s restaurant model can sustain rents that a nationally known brand could not.

An 18-Year Delray Steakhouse Just Went Dark on Atlantic Avenue

Cut 432 was the anniversary dinner, the business deal, the birthday that ran late. It opened in 2010 on Atlantic Avenue in Delray Beach and became one of the street’s defining restaurants — the kind of place where locals could name their server and their server could name their order.

The landlord reportedly sought a rent increase exceeding $100,000. The owners walked away.

Cut 432 is the third Atlantic Avenue restaurant to close in months, following Lefkes Estiatorio and True Vegan. The pattern is not unique to one landlord or one block. It is playing out across the county — independent restaurants that built their neighborhoods are being priced out of them. The Cut 432 ownership has hinted at reopening elsewhere in Delray. If they do, it will be a smaller space at a fraction of the rent.

6 PBC Restaurants Were Shut Down by Health Inspectors

State health inspectors from the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) ordered emergency closures at six Palm Beach County restaurants in recent weeks. All six were cited for rodent and pest violations. All six corrected their issues, passed follow-up inspections, and reopened.

The list:

Two Drunken Goats — Riviera Beach. Three high-priority violations including rodent activity, roach activity, and live flies in the kitchen and dining areas.

Golden Corral — Royal Palm Beach. Emergency closure for pest activity.

Carmine’s La Trattoria — Palm Beach Gardens. Emergency closure for pest activity.

Atlantic Sushi — Delray Beach. Emergency closure for pest activity.

Excell Restaurant — Delray Beach. Emergency closure for pest activity.

Buds Chicken and Seafoods — North Palm Beach. Emergency closure for pest activity.

Emergency closures are temporary. They require the restaurant to halt service, correct the issue, and pass a re-inspection before reopening. All six did so. Summer heat in South Florida drives pests indoors faster than in cooler months, and restaurants with high kitchen volumes are especially vulnerable. The full inspection reports are public record on the DBPR website.

Why Your Favorite Spot Keeps Disappearing

Juliana’s lasted less than a year. Cut 432 lasted 18 years. Both are gone. And the reason is the same both times: the math stopped working.

Here is what has happened across Palm Beach County in 2026 alone:

Oriental Food Market in West Palm Beach — open for more than 40 years. The landlord terminated the lease by email. Closed February 13.

2 Chef’s Souparee in Lake Worth Beach — open for about 20 years. The owner retired at 89 years old. Lines out the door the final week. Closed April 25.

Mathews Brewing Co in Lake Worth Beach — open for about 10 years. The owner turned 62 and walked away. Closed March 13.

Asian Fin in Palm Beach Gardens — the lease expired. Closed March 28.

Ravish Off Ocean in Lantana — summer crowds too thin to cover the overhead. Closed March 14.

The pattern is not bad food. Most of these kitchens were full until their final week. The pattern is rent. Leases signed 15 to 25 years ago are expiring at the same time across the county. Landlords will not renew at the old rates. Insurance has climbed. Labor has climbed. Food costs have climbed. The independent family-run restaurant that signed a lease in 2004 at one rate is now facing a renewal at two or three times that number — and the math does not close.

If you have a family-run favorite, go this month. Tip well. Buy the gift card. Tell your neighbor.

A Netflix Chef Just Picked West Palm Beach

David Chang — the Momofuku founder, two-time James Beard Award winner, and host of multiple Netflix food series including Ugly Delicious — is bringing his fried chicken concept Fuku to 423 S. Rosemary Avenue in downtown West Palm Beach.

The concept centers on one dish: the spicy fried chicken sandwich. It started as a secret off-menu item at Momofuku Noodle Bar in New York. Demand was so high that Chang spun it into its own brand. The original Fuku locations were in Manhattan and the Hudson Yards food hall. The Coral Gables location opened as the first standalone Florida outpost.

West Palm Beach is the follow-up. The address at 423 S. Rosemary Avenue puts it in the Rosemary Square dining corridor, within walking distance of CityPlace and the Brightline station. The target opening is summer 2026.

What to expect: a fast-casual format. Fried chicken sandwiches, chicken fingers, fries, and soft-serve. The original spicy sandwich uses a habanero-brined thigh with pickles and butter on a Martin’s potato roll. Most items are under $15. The menu is small on purpose.

700 Drones Over Delray Beach Tomorrow Night

Delray in the Sky brings about 700 synchronized drones to Old School Square on Wednesday, July 29. The event is part of the America 250 celebration and runs from 6 PM to 9 PM. The drone show itself does not launch until 8:30 PM.

That timing matters. If you arrive at 6, you wait about two and a half warm hours for the actual show. If you arrive at 8, you wait 30 minutes. The payoff happens after sundown.

Details: Old School Square, 51 N. Swinton Avenue, Delray Beach. Free admission. Free parking in city garages off Swinton and Atlantic. No booms — drone shows are quiet, which makes them easy on kids, pets, and the sea turtles nesting on nearby beaches.

If you want to make a night of it, Atlantic Avenue is a short walk east. Dinner first, drone show second.

Wellington’s Amphitheater Reopens Saturday With Free Fleetwood Mac

The amphitheater at 12100 Forest Hill Boulevard has been dark since July 20 for maintenance. Saturday, August 1, it comes back to life with True Rumours — billed as “The Definitive Fleetwood Mac Show.”

Free admission. Food trucks open at 6:30 PM. Music starts at 8 PM. Capacity is about 1,500. Bring chairs or a blanket. Get there by 6 PM if you want a close spot on a reopening night.

Important correction: There are no Thursday Nights in Wellington this week. The amphitheater is closed through July 31. The Thursday concert series resumes August 6 with Life in Technicolor, a Coldplay tribute. If you planned your Thursday around Wellington this week, move it to Saturday.

Coming up: Coldplay tribute August 6. Back-to-School Party in the Park August 7 (free, bounce houses, DJ, food trucks — fills up). The full fall concert calendar brings tributes to the Cars, Dave Matthews Band, Mötley Crüe, and Foreigner. RainoutLine: 561-753-2484.

Back-to-School: The Tax Break, the Supply Giveaways, and the Dates

PBC schools start Monday, August 10. The Florida Back-to-School Sales Tax Holiday runs through August 20. Here are the caps that trip most people up:

Clothing: $100 or less per item — tax-free. A $120 pair of shoes is fully taxed. A $95 pair is free.
School supplies: $50 or less per item.
Learning aids: $30 or less per item.
Computers and laptops: $1,500 or less per item. A $1,200 laptop saves you about $84 in tax.

These caps are per item, not per cart. Online orders count. Excludes theme parks, hotels, and airports.

Free supply giveaways this Saturday, August 1:

Vintage Vibes: Back-to-School Edition — WPB Great Lawn, 100 N. Clematis St, 5-9 PM. Entertainment by Digital Vibez. First 500 pre-registered students get a free supply kit (pickup 5-6 PM).

Back to School Bash — Norman J. Wimbley Gymnasium, 1515 Winfield St, Lake Worth Beach, 9 AM-2 PM. Free backpacks and supplies while they last.

FL Blue Supply Giveaway — 1501 N. Congress Ave, Boynton Beach, 10 AM-1 PM. Free backpacks and supplies, limit 3 per household.

6th Annual Unity Fest — Delray Beach Community Center, 50 NW 1st Ave, 3-9 PM. Free. Family event with food, music, and community resources.

Sea Turtles — Record Season, Hatchlings Emerging

This is the biggest sea turtle nesting season anyone has recorded in Palm Beach County. 575 nests were logged in a single day on June 30. The first-ever olive ridley nest in the state of Florida was confirmed on May 18 at Jupiter Beach — a turtle the University of Georgia research team named “Tini.” Boca Raton has logged 43 leatherback nests this season, beating the previous record of 33 set in 2012. Hatchlings are emerging now across county beaches.
5 beach rules during hatchling season:
1. No flashlights or phone lights on the sand after dark — artificial light sends hatchlings toward roads instead of the ocean.
2. Fill in any holes you dig.
3. Knock down sandcastles before you leave.
4. Take all beach gear off the sand at night.
5. Keep your distance from any marked nest.
