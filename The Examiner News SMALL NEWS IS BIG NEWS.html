P’ville Memorial Softball Tourney Honors Legacy of Beloved Resident

August 1, 2026 Examiner Media

News Based on facts, either observed and verified directly by the reporter, or reported and verified from knowledgeable sources.

We are part of The Trust Project

By Jade D’Agostino, Examiner Reporter Intern

Pleasantville will host an annual softball tournament on Saturday that honors the memory of a longtime beloved resident and raises awareness for mental health struggles.

Community members will gather at Parkway Field from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. for the third annual Jon Lieb Memorial Softball Tournament, celebrating the life of Jon Lieb, a father, husband, coach and friend who died by suicide in June 2023.

“People schlepp here, and just for his memory, his honor,” said his widow, Sarah Lieb.

She has helped to organize the event since it started the year after her husband’s death alongside Paul Dispenza, a close friend of Jon’s, making it a poignant but beloved tradition in Pleasantville during its short history.

“He was a very close friend of mine,” Dispenza said. “He probably was my best friend here. He gave a lot. He made himself available to the community.”

Jon Lieb was a Pleasantville resident for more than 15 years, and a father of two children.

“He was such a strong person and he’s such a positive personality and he was always so fun to be with…He made you feel so warm and special, and he would remember everyone and where their kid went to college; he would remember everything about everything,” Sarah Lieb said.

Lieb said she realized the importance of mental health when her husband was taken from her and her family in a blink of an eye.

She recalled Jon as an extremely friendly, strong and joyful person. Those around him didn’t recognize the signs that he was struggling. She said the experience reinforced the importance of checking in on others whenever possible.

“It can totally happen to anybody,” Lieb said. “One thing said to one person can change something that you just don’t know the trajectory of.”

This year’s event has multiple sponsors as seven softball teams are scheduled to compete.

“It’s going to be sort of a record year of donators, sponsors,” Dispenza said.

After her husband’s passing, Sarah Lieb established the Jon Lieb Legacy Fund as a place to direct the outpouring of donations from friends, family and community members wishing to honor his memory. One of the fund’s first major projects was donating money to Pleasantville High School to build a mobile press box for school athletic events.

The project paid tribute to Jon, who had long served as the announcer for Panther baseball games while sitting at a picnic table with a microphone.

This year, all proceeds from the tournament will benefit Break The Hold, a local organization dedicated to suicide prevention and mental health awareness. Lieb said Break The Hold has helped change the way the community approaches conversations about mental health.

“They were the real ones who started making it something to talk about,” she said.

Lieb said she is grateful to Dispenza for helping make the tournament happen again despite busy summer schedules. While it has previously taken place in June, Dispenza and Lieb moved the event to August 1 as they each had sons graduate from high school this year.

“He’s put blood, sweat and tears into this whole event, and for him to be able to raise money like this and honor Jon at the same time…he’s unbelievable, he’s like a mentor to my boys,” Lieb said.

Dispenza said he’s happy the tournament is continuing for a third year.

“It makes me very proud and happy that we keep doing this and it’s well-received,” he said. “When I say to people, ‘I’m going to do it,’ they’re like, ‘This is great, this is phenomenal that you’re doing this,’ and they make themselves available. They smile. They feel good about thinking of Jon Lieb because this brings him back.”

A large group of people work to organize the event alongside Dispenza and Lieb. Food trucks are set to return, offering lunch options and sweet treats for attendees.

Lieb said the event helps her feel connected to the community.

“It makes me feel that we just picked the right place to raise our family…This is a testament to every single person in town and all the friends that he made and that my kids have made and the friendship that we’ve developed with all these families,” Lieb said. “It’s an unbelievable way to honor him and his memory.”

At last year’s tournament, Lieb’s two sons released balloons on first base, the position Jon played for the softball team he founded, The Moonlight Grahams.

“He was huge in softball,” Dispenza said. “You know, he was like the king of putting together teams, recruiting players, playing in multiple leagues, he was really dedicated to softball.”

Jon Lieb’s sons also traditionally throw out the tournament’s first pitch, a particularly touching moment.

Wrist bands, beer koozies and T-shirts are distributed, all designed to bring awareness toward mental health.

Dispenza said his experience coordinating the tournaments and raising money has made him realize the importance of this work.

“The last three years have made me think about how I could do more,” he said.

While Sarah Lieb and her family still struggle with the tragedy, they’re putting their efforts into helping others navigate similar situations.

“My little guy was like ‘Mom, if one person’s life is changed by this, like one talk at a high school, one talk at a college, that means we made it,’” she said. “That daddy didn’t leave for no reason. He had a purpose.”

Jade D’Agostino is a Robert Schork Memorial intern for The Examiner this summer. A student at Penn State, she serves as a reporter for The Daily Collegian, where she specializes in investigative journalism.
