    By Metroland Staff

    Kawartha Art Gallery has chosen a timely theme for its annual juried exhibition, inviting artists to submit work that speaks to democracy, participation and social change.

    “Voices & Visibility: Women, Art, and Political Change” aims to explore the evolving relationship between democracy, participation, and the role of women in shaping communities through creative expression.

    More than an annual juried exhibition, “Voices & Visibility” marks an important evolution for the Lindsay-based gallery.

    As Kawartha Art Gallery enters a fresh chapter at its new home at 19 Cambridge St. S. in Lindsay, it is expanding its curatorial vision through exhibitions that empower artists to engage with contemporary issues, encourage meaningful public dialogue, and demonstrate the power of art to connect communities.

    Running from Oct. 7 to Dec. 19, “Voices & Visibility” welcomes submissions interpreting themes of democracy, advocacy, leadership, identity, protest, belonging, representation, human rights, and community engagement.

    Meaningful community dialogue

    “Artists have long understood the transformative power of creative expression, and science is now affirming what they have known for generations” said gallery executive director Susan Taylor in a media release.

    “A growing body of research demonstrates that participation in the arts can improve mental health, reduce loneliness, strengthen social connection, and enhance overall well-being. This year’s Annual Juried Exhibition reflects that evidence, inviting visitors to experience art not only as a form of creative expression, but as a catalyst for connection, understanding, and meaningful community dialogue.”

    Submissions may include, but are not limited to, any paint medium, sculpture, photography, fibre art and digital media. Artists are encouraged to draw from personal experience, historical events, contemporary issues, or imagined futures to create work that inspires reflection and conversation.

    The exhibition also builds on the momentum created by the gallery’s recent “Empty Seats” show and community discussion, which demonstrated participants’ appetite for thoughtful conversations about civic participation and representation. “Voices & Visibility” broadens that conversation through creative expression.

    Honouring achievement

    To recognize artistic excellence, the gallery will present $2,000 in cash awards, including the $1,000 Ken Found Memorial Award for Highest Achievement.

    Presented annually, the award recognizes the artist whose work demonstrates the highest level of artistic excellence while most effectively reflecting the theme and intent of the Annual Juried Exhibition.

    Established through the Ken Found Art Fund, the award celebrates Found’s enduring legacy of championing local artists and enriching the cultural life of the Kawartha Lakes community.

    The exhibition is open to Ontario artists aged 18 and older working in painting, drawing, printmaking, mixed media, sculpture, fibre art, photography, and digital art.

    Online submissions close Sept. 28. The awards reception will be held Nov. 7.

    For more information, visit kawarthagallery.com.
