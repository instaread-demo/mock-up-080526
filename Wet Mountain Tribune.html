5th annual Day of the Cowboy celebration brings poetry and music to the Valley

July 25, 2026

The history of the cowboy, especially that of Cowboy Poetry, traces back to the mid-1800s. Poetry emerged as a sanctioned pastime for cowboys as they traversed the open prairies of Texas to Kansas during the cattle drives. With little entertainment except their voices and those around them, Cowboy Poetry became, and still is, a distinct genre of verse meant for preserving the stories of cowboy life. 

Originating in 2005, The National Day of the Cowboy recognizes the historic contributions of the American cowboy and cowgirl every fourth Saturday in July. While events are held at museums and heritage sites across the United States, five years ago Dennis Russell and JJ Steele decided the Valley community deserved such an event as well.

Russell and Steele met more than a decade ago at a cowboy gathering in La Junta, Colo. Since then, the two have moved to the Valley on separate occasions “so it’s interesting that we developed this friendship just…circumstantially around cowboy music and poetry,” Steele said. Both Russell and Steele have their distinctive skill sets to put on such an event, “I have mine and he has his,” Steele added, “Dennis is the purse strings. He is.” 

1

In 2014, Russell was appointed the first Poet Laureate of Cimarron, New Mexico and founded the Cimarron Cowboy Music & Poetry Gathering. Now, Russell, along with his wife, Beverly Gray Russell, comprise The Russellers. However, this time last year, Beverly fell, injured her neck, and required surgery. 

“We didn’t play for a whole year,” Russell continued, until just last month when the two performed together again for the first time at the 3rd Street Gallery’s June opening reception. Eager to be performing together again, the duo will accompany Peggy Malone, JJ Steele, Jimmy Cantwell, Floyd Beard, Joel Alden White and Terry Nash to bring the 2026 Day of the Cowboy’s celebration to life. 

“We hope to have this event strong, no matter what this fire does,” Russell said in regard to the Aspen Acres Fire. “But really it’s important to know what [local ranchers] are going through right now,” because as the Day of the Cowboy celebration quickly approaches, the Aspen Acres Fire remains a threat to many local ranchers. 

Not just a threat to the land, but to cattle as well. A week ago Friday, Russell explained, “I woke up and had a whole other herd of cattle on my place that had broke out from Highway 165 and come over the ridge.” As the fire grew closer, the cows found solitude on Russell’s land, “and that’s just kind of how things go. When God sends us things like this that change our lives, there’s more to it.”

The fire’s present destruction centralizes the cowboy’s stewardship to this land. Cowboys made poetry to preserve a segment of American culture and their stewardship to the Earth — the “cowboy was a good steward,” Steele said. “[Cowboys] marked a lot of history down by putting it in a poem, by putting it to rhyme and meter,” Russell added, “[so] they could remember that there was a pass or there was a crossing.”

Regardless of time, an unbroken oral chain of poetry has survived generations of poets and musicians who “romanticize the mystery of it all,” Russell remarked — of life as a cowboy. Steele added, the “American cowboy is an icon of resilience and independence.”

Each performer is acclaimed for their distinct approach to cowboy celebrations.

Cartwell “is new to the area,” Steele explained. “With a big cowboy voice,” Russell added. 

Malone “is just a fireball,” Steele said. “She grabs [her guitar] and starts wailing on [it].”

“Joel White is a…I guess the term is camp,” Steele said. “The kind of stuff you’d hear around the campfire,” Steele added, “he’s just really singing some…good, earthy cowboy stuff.”

Beard will return again this year after his absence at last year’s Day of the Cowboy celebration. “[He] is multi winner of awards” Steele said, and also contributes to The Fence Post, a nationwide agricultural newspaper.

To round out this year’s performers, Nash will emcee this year’s celebration as one of the most sought-out Cowboy poets around.

The satisfaction of bringing a cultural event like this to the Valley community is what Russell and Steele feel is the most rewarding aspect of doing this year after year. “As far as I know, we’re going to be here doing this until something calls us away,” Steele chuckled.  

Mark your calendars for the 5th Annual Day of the Cowboy Celebration at 2 p.m. on July 25 at the All Aboard Westcliffe Event Center. 

Tickets are $20 and can be purchased in advance through JJ Steele at 719-371-2013.

– Brett LeVan
